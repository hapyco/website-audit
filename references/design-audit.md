# Design & UX review

Run this review for every website audit. Use the homepage, a representative service page and the main conversion path. Inspect desktop and mobile. Review the original ten audit areas as well.

## Checks

- Accessibility: contrast, zoom settings, keyboard access, visible focus, control names, meaningful image text, heading order, and labels or errors on available forms. Do not submit client forms. An automated score does not prove accessibility compliance. Identify any check that could not be completed in private evidence.
- Grids and blocks: compare container widths, left/right alignment, columns, gutters, block order and stacking. Check for overflow, clipped content and unusually wide or narrow text. Record page and viewport for dimensional findings.
- Buttons and controls: check label clarity, destination, action, click area, visual priority, focus/hover states and consistent styles. Confirm phone labels match dial links. Check whether the primary journey reaches a usable next step.
- Typography and sizing: inspect font families, heading/body hierarchy, actual sizes, line height, line length, wrapping and readability. A font choice alone is not a usability defect.
- Spacing and padding: inspect section gaps, card padding, gutters, alignment and repeated components. Distinguish measured inconsistency from a subjective preference. Record working patterns as well as defects.
- Color contrast: record text/background colors and font size/weight. Account for the visible background rather than transparent CSS alone. Use the relevant current reference. Verify automated findings in the rendered control. Save ratios, affected elements and screenshots.
- UI/UX mistakes: inspect navigation, competing or misleading CTAs, dead ends, missing feedback, placeholder blocks, unavailable forms and inconsistent contact routes. Describe the observed interruption and its effect on the buyer journey.

## Client section

Use one heading: Design & UX. Combine related observations into 2-3 main findings; do not publish the checklist as separate categories. Lead with the defects that affect understanding or enquiry completion. State the observed fact, affected element and user consequence. Use dimensions or ratios where they clarify the issue. Include what works when it provides useful context.

Example: "The two service rows misalign by 64 px at a 1280 px viewport, and white heading text disappears against white. Two orange CTAs measure 3.29:1 contrast against a 4.5:1 reference." Use only the prospect's verified values.

Keep the tone findings-led. Do not assign design tasks to the prospect. Avoid generic statements such as "improve typography", "modernize the design" or "optimize UX". Do not claim a complete accessibility certification or invent an overall design score. Reuse conversion and mobile findings without repeating them across the client report.
